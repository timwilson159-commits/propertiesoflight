First, the basics

What actually is light?

  • Light is a form of energy that travels in straight lines called rays
  • It travels at about 300,000 km/s, the fastest speed in the universe
  • When a ray hits matter, one of four things happens: it is absorbed, reflected, refracted, or scattered
Watch a ray travel

A ray of light has nothing to slow it down until it hits something.

Colour is made of light

White light is every colour, mixed

  • White light is actually all colours mixed together
  • Mixing red, green and blue light makes white: this is additive mixing
  • Mixing paint works the opposite way: it subtracts colour
Discuss: why do TV and phone screens use tiny red, green and blue lights instead of paint?

Reflection

When light bounces back

Reflection

Light bounces off a surface

  • Light bounces off a surface instead of passing through it
  • Law of reflection: angle of incidence = angle of reflection, measured from the normal (a line at 90° to the surface)
  • Specular reflection: smooth surface, sharp clear image (a mirror)
  • Diffuse reflection: rough surface, scattered image (paper, a wall)

Refraction

When light changes direction

Refraction

Light bends when it changes speed

  • Light bends when it crosses into a material where it travels at a different speed
  • Slows down entering a denser medium: bends towards the normal
  • Speeds up leaving a denser medium: bends away from the normal
  • How much it bends depends on the material's refractive index

Absorption

When light disappears into heat

Absorption

Absorbed light becomes heat

  • Some light energy is absorbed by matter and changed into heat
  • Light that isn't absorbed is reflected: that's the colour you see
  • A red apple reflects red light and absorbs all the other colours
  • Black objects absorb the most and heat up fastest; white objects reflect the most and stay coolest

Scattering

When light sprays off in every direction

Scattering

Small particles, scattered light

  • Light bounces off tiny particles or gas molecules in many directions at once
  • Shorter wavelengths (blue/violet) scatter more than longer wavelengths (red/orange): this is Rayleigh scattering
  • At sunset, light passes through more atmosphere, so blue is scattered away before it reaches you, leaving orange and red
